# Frame: market size, competition, platforms and personalisation, and their names

Step 1 of the review, read on 2026-11-04. It states the problem, gives the field's names beside the practitioners', and says whether the repository's name matches.

**Shape:** [frame](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/c43f2770000a9af5c2aa3cbc6f997da7a7e13b34/shapes/frame.md)

## The problem in one sentence

How big a market a venture can reach before it has sales, what of its difference lasts against rivals, whether it prices two sides, and what tailoring does to demand, cost and rivalry.

## What the field calls it

| Level | The field's name | First met in |
|---|---|---|
| the whole | **industrial organisation**, IO: the structure of markets, entry and the conduct of firms | Tirole 1988 |
| a size from parts or from a whole | **top-down and bottom-up forecasting**, in a **hierarchy** of series; **market potential** in diffusion models | Athanasopoulos 2009; Van den Bulte 1997 |
| a quantity judged by its parts | **judgmental decomposition** | MacGregor 1994 |
| the width of a guess | **overconfidence in interval estimates** | Soll 2004 |
| the number of rivals and the room for them | **entry threshold**; **market structure**; **entry** | Bresnahan 1991; Geroski 1995 |
| a difference between goods | **product differentiation**: horizontal, along a line of tastes, and vertical, in quality | Aspremont 1979; Tirole 1988 |
| what a rival cannot copy | **sustainable competitive advantage**; **barriers to imitation**; **causal ambiguity** | Reed 1990 |
| how much the industry explains | **variance decomposition** of returns: industry, corporate and business effects | Rumelt 1991; McGahan 1997 |
| the cost of changing supplier | **switching costs**; **lock-in** | Klemperer 1995 |
| a platform and its sides | **two-sided markets**; **multi-sided platforms**; **price structure** | Rochet 2003; Armstrong 2006; Rysman 2009 |
| a good made to one person | **mass customization**; **product variety**; **behavior-based personalization**; **choice overload** | Silveira 2001; Zhang 2011p; Scheibehenne 2010 |

## What practice calls it

- **Total addressable market**, TAM, with the serviceable and the obtainable market inside it: the size of a market, in a pitch (Damodaran 2014; Gurley 2014). The field has no such triple; the nearest is a market potential and a share.
- **Moat**: a lasting advantage, scored for how long returns above the cost of capital last (Mauboussin 2016). The field's word is sustainable competitive advantage.
- **Five forces**: rivals, customers, suppliers, entrants and substitutes (Porter 2008). The field's frame is market structure.
- **Network effects** and **marketplace**: a platform seen from its sides (Eisenmann 2006).
- **Mass customization** and **personalization**: tailoring at the price of a standard good (Pine 1993; McKinsey 2021).

## Does the repository's name match?

- Partly. "Market" is the practitioners' word. The field's is industrial organisation for structure and entry, and forecasting for size.
- The evidence topic is named market-structure. The repository keeps its name; the maintainer decides ([record 0002](../../decisions/0002-keep-the-name-and-size-a-market-by-two-methods.md)).
