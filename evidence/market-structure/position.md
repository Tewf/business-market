# Position

Step 4 of the review. What is known, what was not found, and what this repository asks. Each "not found" is a statement about these searches, not about the world. Terms are in [frame](frame.md).

**Shape:** [position](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/c43f2770000a9af5c2aa3cbc6f997da7a7e13b34/shapes/position.md)

## Known

- A size built from parts and a size cut from a whole can differ by a factor of 25 on one premise (Damodaran 2014; Gurley 2014).
- On series of sales, splitting by forecast proportions and combining levels beat the simple methods (Athanasopoulos 2009).
- A ceiling fitted to early sales is unreliable (Van den Bulte 1997), and stated intervals are too narrow (Soll 2004).
- Industry explains a minority of the variance in returns; the business explains more (Rumelt 1991; McGahan 1997).
- Competition changes quickly with the first few rivals in a local market (Bresnahan 1991).
- A two-sided market has two conditions, and its price structure often subsidises one side (Rysman 2009; Kaiser 2006).
- More variety has a demand value and two kinds of cost, and choice overload is virtually zero on average (Brynjolfsson 2003; Randall 2001; Scheibehenne 2010).

## Not found in these searches

- A measured error of a venture's market size, top-down or bottom-up, against later sales (F26, F27).
- A comparison of the two methods for a new market, as against series of existing sales (F18, F19).
- A field study of personalisation and switching costs or rivalry (F13, F24).
- A count of how many ventures use each sizing method or each competition frame.
- The result of Dangerfield 1992 and the findings of the located-only surveys.

## The questions

| Question | Answer from the evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 How far apart do the sizes fall, and why? | in the one pair found, 25 times, on whether the product expands the market; no measured gap-to-error link | convention, untested |
| Q2 What differences last? | business-specific advantages are large and stable; few new establishments last five years; what rivals cannot trace to a cause is harder to copy | measured for the variance split; convention for the rest |
| Q3 When is it two-sided, and what does it change? | when an intermediary sits between two groups that affect each other; pricing may subsidise one side; the rule depends on how users differ | supported with caveat |
| Q4 What does tailoring do? | adds demand value and two costs; may erode differentiation when rivals use the data; field effect on rivalry not found | measured for demand and cost; convention for rivalry |

## What this repository asks

- How far a bottom-up interval, with its inputs tagged as guesses, covers the sales a venture later makes.
- Whether the gap between the two methods predicts that coverage.
- Whether the moat words of practice, such as brand and scale, name differences that survive a rival who sees them.

## What the shapes must decide

- A design document that fixes one named example market, the sources of its inputs and the first sale that closes the loop.
- Whether the program takes a fourth input, a launch time, or keeps the yearly amount of money only.
