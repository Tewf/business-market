# State of the art

Step 3 of the review. For each question, the best evidence, kept apart from the practice most adopted. Upper bounds (what was achieved) are kept apart from limits (what was shown not to work). Sources are in [review](review.md).

**Shape:** [state of the art](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/c43f2770000a9af5c2aa3cbc6f997da7a7e13b34/shapes/state-of-the-art.md)

## Q1 How far apart do top-down and bottom-up sizes fall, and which assumptions explain the gap?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | Athanasopoulos 2009 | on a hierarchy of tourism series, a split by forecast proportions and an optimal combination did best of five methods (abstract, secondary) | measured |
| Upper bound | MacGregor 1994 | splitting a quantity into parts improved accuracy on nine problems with extreme, uncertain targets (abstract, secondary) | measured |
| Limit | Van den Bulte 1997 | a ceiling fitted to early sales is biased, enough to make long-range prediction problematic (abstract, secondary) | measured |
| Limit | Soll 2004 | stated intervals miss the answer far more often than their confidence says (abstract, secondary) | measured |
| Most adopted | Damodaran 2014 | one top-down figure, a summed market times a share; Gurley 2014 shows the premise it hides | convention, untested |

## Q2 Which differences can a rival copy, and what does the literature say lasts?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | McGahan 1997 | about 19% of the variance in profit is industry and 32% business-specific (abstract, secondary) | measured |
| Upper bound | Bresnahan 1991 | competitive conduct changes quickly as incumbents grow in number (abstract, secondary) | measured |
| Limit | Mauboussin 2016 | about half of new establishments survive five years; fade is faster with high returns and heavy investment (read) | measured, descriptive |
| Most adopted | Porter 2008 | the five forces; no adoption count found | convention, untested |

## Q3 When is a venture a two-sided market, and what does that change in how it prices?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | Kaiser 2006 | magazine reader prices are subsidised and advertisers pay most (abstract, secondary) | measured |
| Best evidence | Rysman 2009 | two conditions: an intermediary, and an externality from each set of agents to the other (abstract, secondary) | supported with caveat |
| Upper bound | Rochet 2003 | a model of the allocation of prices between sides for competing platforms (abstract, secondary) | convention, argued from a model |
| Limit | Weyl 2010 | what that model implies turns on how users differ (abstract, secondary) | convention, argued from a model |
| Most adopted | Eisenmann 2006 | two-sided networks often return more at scale; no figures; opening read | convention, untested |
